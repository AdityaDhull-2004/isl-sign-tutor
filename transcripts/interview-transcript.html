Turn 3

4. Each clip is cut to the part where the signer is actually signing and resampled to 32 frames. Each frame has 67 points: 25 upper-body points plus 21 per hand. Each point gives x, y and their velocity, which is 268 numbers. A hand-local copy of the 42 hand points adds 168 more: 268 + 168 = 436. What normalization does is that each frame is centred on the shoulder midpoint and divided by the clip's median shoulder width. So where the signer stands, and how far they are from the camera, doesn't matter. An additional copy of the hand-local block is used to analyse the hand movements separately as there are very small handmovements (fingers, wrists etc) relative to the arm that need proper analysis as they are smaller compared to the body.

5. The pre-training didn't help because hiding 4-8 frames is not much of a problem as the linear interpolation between these frames is sufficient to still unedrstand the sign. The numbers that showed this are that the trained encoders reach a loss of 0.041, against 0.055 for plain linear interpolation.

Round 2 scores

Q4: 9/10. The derivation of 436 and the normalisation are both correct.

  • Be more exact about the hand-local block: each hand is re-expressed relative to its own wrist and divided by palm size. That gives finger shape independent of where the arm is.
  • Add if asked: only x and y are used. A missing point is interpolated over time, and a hand never seen in the clip becomes zeros. The signing part is kept from when a wrist rises above a rest line, with 0.25 s of padding.

Q5: 8/10. The reason and the 0.041 vs 0.055 numbers are right.

  • Add the time scale: 4–8 frames is only 0.3–0.6 s, and hands move smoothly over that, so a straight line fills most of it.
  • Admit the evidence is thin: only one completed trial (trial 13) started from a pre-trained encoder. It reached 95.20% against 95.80% from scratch. The winning 320×2 size had no matching pre-trained encoder.
  • Say what you'd try next: longer masked spans, masking a whole hand, or contrastive learning.

Q6: 0/10. Here's the answer to learn:

  • Why add the weaker graph network: it makes different mistakes. It works over the skeleton graph, while the transformer works over time. The ablation shows it:
    • 1 transformer: 95.10%
    • 2 transformers: 95.25% (+0.15)
    • 2 transformers + the graph network: 95.54% (+0.29)

    The graph network added about twice what the second transformer did. It has only 578K parameters, and its 0.30 weight was chosen on val.

  • Why the student only matches one transformer: it's a code issue found in review. AWP starts at 30% of training, and after that each update uses only plain cross-entropy at the perturbed weights. So the distillation term stops shaping the student for the last 70% of training, and it ends up as one transformer trained normally (95.10%). The fix would be to compute the distillation loss at the perturbed weights too. Say plainly that you found it and haven't fixed it.

Total: 36/60. Before the interview, fix three things: be able to say exactly how each split was used (Q3), answer the "is it noise?" question with p ≈ 0.07 (Q2), and learn Q6.

The two questions you skipped, short version:

  1. How does the corrector work?
    • Method:
      • It aligns the attempt with banded DTW against 6–10 native renditions of the same word.
      • It computes six distances: handshape, palm, fingers, location, movement size and movement shape.
      • Each score is the distance to the nearest 3 natives divided by how much the natives themselves vary.
      • Thresholds are set on val, so that 5% of correct signs get a "fix" and 10% get a "tip".
    • Results: 9.5% false alarms on correct test signs. Caught: handshape 97.7%, second hand 100%, location 87.0%, movement reversed 78.7%, movement shrunk 73.8%, palm 71.0%.
    • Weak spots: palm orientation, because one camera can't see depth well. The mistakes were simulated, and no ISL teacher has checked the feedback.
  2. How is it deployed?
    • Server: an EC2 CPU server in Sydney, not Lambda like your other projects. It handles a live WebSocket video stream and keeps MediaPipe and Ollama running.
    • Access: password-protected because the FDMSE licence forbids public hosting, so no videos are uploaded and references play as skeletons. It uses HTTPS (needed for the browser to allow the webcam) with wss://.
    • Wake on visit: waking takes 23–30 s, and it stops after 20 min idle.
    • Bottlenecks: on the laptop it's MediaPipe (7–9 fps; recognition takes only 8–24 ms of each 107–149 ms frame). On the hosted version it's the network round trip, about 0.3 s per frame, hidden by keeping 3 frames in flight.
